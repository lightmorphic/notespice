Privacy

How this website handles information about you.

The short version

This website collects nothing about you. There is no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no cookies and no forms to fill in. Two servers see your IP address in their ordinary logs, as any web server does, and both are described below. Nothing else about you is recorded.

The editor you can try on the home page

The working editor on the home page runs entirely inside your own browser. The notes you type into it are never sent anywhere, are not saved between visits, and disappear when you close the tab.

What the host records

The site is a set of static files served by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub records ordinary server log information when a page is requested, including your IP address, so that it can deliver the page and protect the service from abuse. That processing is GitHub's, not ours, and we cannot see those logs or turn them off. GitHub's lawful basis for it is its own legitimate interest in running and securing the service. Their privacy statement covers what they do with it.

The app menu in the corner

The grid button at the top of the page lists Lightmorphic's other work. Its script and icons come from apps.lightmorphic.com, which is our own server. Loading them means that server also records your IP address in its ordinary logs. It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, and does not track you between visits or between sites.

The Notespice software itself

Notespice is software you run on your own machine. When you run it, your notes are files on your own disk. They are never sent to us, and the software makes no outbound connections of its own.

Your rights

UK data protection law gives you the right to ask what personal data an organisation holds about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to complain. We hold none, so in practice there is nothing to send you, but you are welcome to ask and we will confirm that in writing.

Write to privacy@lightmorphic.com. If you are not happy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
